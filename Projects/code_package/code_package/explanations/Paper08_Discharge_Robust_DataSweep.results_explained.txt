Table 4 shows that all three learners beat the rule: DP by +1.03 ± 0.17 (p < 0.001), Monte Carlo by +1.17
± 0.18 (p < 0.001) and Q-learning by +1.04 ± 0.16 (p < 0.001); the oracle DP reaches -4.29. The learners
are statistically indistinguishable from each other. The mechanism is visible in the other columns: DP
shortens the stay from 4.79 to 2.48 days and readmissions rise from 6.2% to 10.0%, with follow-up used
in 59% of stays rather than 47%. Accounting for the weights, 2.32 days saved is worth 2.32, the extra
readmissions cost 0.95 and the extra follow-ups 0.24, a net of about +1.12 (undiscounted; the measured
difference of +1.03 is discounted at 0.99). The gain is entirely a consequence of the price list: the same
DP policy would stop beating the rule if one readmission were worth more than about 55 days of stay
rather than 25. A health system that regards a 61% relative rise in readmissions as unacceptable would
not adopt this policy whatever its return.

Table 6 puts numbers on this. The paired comparison of robust DP (c = 2) with nominal DP gives -0.22 ±
0.20 in the worst case (p = 0.074) and +0.08 ± 0.20 at the training risk (p = 0.437): neither is significant,
and the worst-case point estimate is unfavourable to robust DP. Over the radii the worst-case return runs
from -8.10 (nominal) through -8.11, -8.17, -8.32 to -8.44 (c = 4), a monotone decline whose end points
differ by 0.34; the intervals are about ±0.29, so only the largest differences are distinguishable. The
readmission rate at the training risk also rises monotonically with the radius, from 10.8% (nominal) to
13.7% (c = 4). This is consistent with the design property noted in Section 5: pessimism applied to the
extend action alone makes staying look worse and pushes the policy toward earlier discharge, which
exposes more patients to readmission. We did not record stay lengths for the robust variants, so this
explanation is untested.

Figure 3 adds a stability check, which also goes against the usual argument for robustness. Under
episode-level bootstrap resampling of one replicate's data, nominal DP reproduces its full-data policy in
89% ± 4% of well-visited states (mean ± s.d.), whereas robust DP (c = 2) does so in 80% ± 5%. Robust
DP is less, not more, stable by this measure. A single data draw was used, so the comparison is
descriptive.

Table 7 and Figure 4 are where robustness earns its keep, within limits. With unchanged risk, robust DP
(c = 2) beats nominal DP by +0.69 ± 0.19 at 200 episodes (p < 0.001) and +0.67 ± 0.32 at 400 (p =
0.005), the benefit shrinks to +0.11 ± 0.06 at 1000 (p = 0.010) and is -0.01 ± 0.11 at 3000 (p = 0.821). At
the Bonferroni threshold of 0.0063 over the 8 sweep comparisons, the sizes with a significant gain are
200, 400 episodes; 200, 400, 1000 are nominally significant. Robust c = 1 is in between, for example -
5.90 against -6.38 nominal at 200 episodes (not tested). The gain at 1,000 episodes in this sweep (+0.11)
is consistent in sign with the main comparison at the same data size (+0.08 ± 0.20, p = 0.437), a separate
set of replicates in which it was not significant. H2 is therefore supported, but only for unchanged risk.
Under a 1.5-fold shift the difference is +0.05, +0.03, -0.08 and -0.10 for the four sizes, with every
interval containing zero (smallest p-value 0.33). Robustness bought with scarce data does not carry over
to a shift in readmission risk.
Why does pessimism help at small samples and not at large? A plausible, untested account is that
nominal DP built from few stays is distorted by the support constraint: pairs seen fewer than ten times
are completed with the worst observed reward, so rarely logged discharge actions look terrible and the
planner over-extends stays; transition-side pessimism on extending counteracts this and moves the policy
toward the earlier discharges that the benchmark rewards. As data grow every pair is supported, radii
shrink as one over the square root of n_sa and the two planners coincide. If this account is right, the
benefit is a correction of a small-sample artefact of the pipeline, not robustness to the world.
